WEEK 19
Recovery Before Peak · Frequency Held, Load Cut · Nico
Frequency 2× / week
Days Mon + Wed
Duration ~30 min
When After running
Sets 2 per exercise
Rest 90s between sets
⚡ Warm-up · 5 min
Easy bike or jog 5 min → 10 leg swings each direction per leg → 10 bodyweight squats → banded lateral walk (exercise 1) as activation. Recovery week. The frequency does not change — the load does. Two sessions, Monday and Wednesday: Monday stacks after the easy 5 km, Wednesday stands alone. Both run the same grid below, and everything drops to 2 sets at roughly 60% of week 18's load. This is the week your connective tissue catches up with the biggest block you have ever run — week 18 was 62 km and 2,250 m, with 2,050 m of descending across Friday and Saturday alone. Tendons and fascia adapt on a slower clock than muscle, and this is the only week between here and the race that gives them that time. Do not use the free energy to lift heavier. If you finish a session feeling you could have done much more, the week is working exactly as designed.
🗓 Why the gym doesn't get cut
A recovery week cuts volume, not habits. Your own experience is that two strength sessions a week are what keep you healthy, so the frequency holds even here — what comes down is sets and kilos. This week was originally written with a single light gym day; the second session is back because skipping is how people walk into a peak block with a de-conditioned posterior chain and pick up the injury the recovery week was supposed to prevent. Short, easy sessions. In and out in 30 minutes.
🗓 What this week is protecting
Look at what sits on the other side of this week: week 20 is 69 km / 2,600 m, week 21 is 75 km / 2,900 m — the peak of the entire plan — and week 22 is 65 km / 2,200 m. Three weeks back to back, each bigger than anything you have done. This is the last absorption week before them, and there is no second chance at it. Everything you hold back now is available in three weeks' time; everything you spend here comes straight out of week 21. Legs should feel progressively fresher across the week, not flat. If they still feel heavy by Sunday, tell me before week 20 starts — that is a conversation worth having early, not on the Saturday of a 2,600 m week.
Exercises · Monday + Wednesday
1
Banded
Lateral Walk
band · above knees
2 × 12
steps / side
Approximate reference (seated band hip abduction) — same muscle and same band resistance, but yours is standing and walking, which loads the stance leg as well
Target: abductors
Position
  • Band just above the knees, hands on hips
  • Quarter-squat, chest up, feet pointing straight ahead
Execution
  • Step sideways with control, keeping constant tension on the band
  • Resist the trailing leg back in — don't let it snap across
  • Small steps, stay low, no torso lean over the stance leg
  • Week 19: back above the knees, 2 × 12 steps per side — drop the ankle position for one week. This one is activation, so it stays in both sessions regardless of the recovery
  • It's first on purpose — this is warm-up activation, not a lift; go straight from here into the split squat. It costs four minutes and no fatigue, and it is the cheapest insurance you have against a tired glute medius letting the knee fall inward in week 21
Watch: feet turning outward · knees caving inward · standing up out of the quarter-squat. If the knees collapse, the band is too strong — go lighter
2
Bulgarian
Split Squat
6 kg
2 × 8
/ leg
Approximate reference (dumbbell single-leg split squat) — this dataset has no Bulgarian split squat; the pattern is close, the setup differs
Target: quads
Position
  • Back foot on the bench, laces down
  • Front foot forward — knee doesn't pass the toes
Execution
  • Lower until the thigh is ≈ parallel to the floor
  • Torso slightly leaned forward
  • Push through the whole foot, mainly the heel
  • Week 19: 6 kg × 8 reps, 2 sets — back to the lighter kettlebell for one week, down from week 18's 12 kg. Quality of position over everything; this is a movement rehearsal, not a training stimulus. 12 kg returns in week 20
Watch: knee caving inward · weight on the back leg · front foot too close. Reps 9 and 10 are where form breaks — stop the set rather than finish it badly
3
Single-Leg
Calf Raise
bodyweight
2 × 8
/ leg
Target: calves
Position
  • Ball of the foot on the edge of a step, heel in the air
  • Hold something for balance, not to support your weight
Execution
  • Rise all the way up, squeeze at the top for 1 sec
  • Lower in 4 seconds — slow eccentric
  • Heel below the step for full range of motion
  • Week 19: bodyweight, 4s eccentric, 2 sets — the volume halves. Your calves and Achilles have just absorbed 2,050 m of braking in week 18, which is far more eccentric work than any kettlebell can give them; the job now is to keep the tissue used to the movement while it repairs, not to fatigue it again. If anything in the Achilles is still talking from week 18, do one set instead of two — that is the correct call this week, not a failure
Watch: lowering too fast — brake more than you think you need to. Any Achilles soreness lasting past 48h → cut the volume for the week
4
Single-Leg
Glute Bridge
6 kg
2 × 8
/ leg
Target: glutes
Position
  • Lying on your back, one knee bent, heel close to your glutes
  • The other leg straight or with the knee pulled to your chest
Execution
  • Week 19: 6 kg, 2 sec hold, 2 sets — lighter kettlebell, shorter squeeze, down from week 18's 12 kg. Hips still level; the standard for what counts as a rep never deloads
  • Drive through the heel of the supporting leg, raise your hip
  • Hips level — don't let the free side drop
  • Squeeze the glute, hold 2 sec at the top
  • Ribs down — don't arch your lower back
  • If the weight breaks hip level, drop back to 6 kg — form rules
Watch: the free-side hip drops or rotates → shorten the range and level out before continuing. Drive from the glute, never the lower back
5
Dead
Bug
bodyweight
2 × 8
/ side
Target: abs
Position
  • Lying on your back, arms straight up toward the ceiling
  • Knees at 90° over your hips (tabletop)
Execution
  • Lower back pressed to the floor — the key point
  • Slowly extend the opposite arm + leg
  • Without touching the floor, return, switch sides
  • Flat back > range of motion
  • Week 19: 4 sec per rep, 2 sets. Trunk control is cheap to maintain and expensive to rebuild, so the tempo holds even though the volume halves. It costs you nothing and it is what keeps your form together in hour five of week 21
Watch: this is anti-extension control, not strength — adding weight recruits the psoas and arches your back. Back arching as you extend the leg → reduce the range
6
Side
Plank
bodyweight
2 × 30s
/ side
Approximate reference (incline side plank variant) — this dataset has no plain side-plank hold
Target: abs
Position
  • Side plank on your forearm
  • Body in a straight line from head to feet
  • Hips stacked, not rotated or dropped
  • Bottom leg on the floor, top leg stacked on top
Execution
  • Contract your obliques and core, keep a straight line
  • Breathe, don't hold your breath
  • Week 19: 30 sec — back down from week 18's 45. It returns to 45 in week 20 and grows from there
  • Cut the time short if your hips drop — form > duration
Watch: hips dropping or rotating — cut the time before your form breaks
7
High Step-up
+ Eccentric Descent
bodyweight
2 × 8
/ leg
Approximate reference (dumbbell step-up) — shows the step-up pattern only; doesn't capture the box height or slow eccentric descent that define this exercise
Target: glutes
Position
  • Use the jump box — height at mid-shin / knee
  • Whole foot planted on top, not just the toes
Execution
  • Step up driving through the heel of the top leg, no push-off from the bottom leg
  • Drive the knee up — mimics the power-hike motion on a climb
  • Knee tracking over the foot — not caving inward
  • Lower in 4 seconds — the slow eccentric preps your quads for trail descents
  • Week 19: bodyweight on a lower box, 2 sets. Still no load, and now less height too — week 18 took the box back to full, and this week it steps down again. Saturday is 18 km / 700 m, the easiest mountain day in a month, so the gym isn't competing with it — but a recovery week is not the moment to introduce a new stimulus. Full height returns in week 20
Watch: the value is in the slow descent and the higher box, not stepping up fast. Knee pain → lower the height or reduce the range
8
One-Arm
Bent-Over Row
6 kg
2 × 10
/ arm
Target: upper back
Position
  • Hinge at the hips, flat back, soft knees
  • Free hand on a bench or your thigh for support
  • Kettlebell hangs straight down from the working arm
Execution
  • Pull the kettlebell to your hip, elbow close to the body
  • Squeeze the shoulder blade at the top
  • Lower under control, don't let it drop
  • Week 19: 6 kg × 10 reps, 2 sets — lighter and shorter. Back to 12 kg × 12 in week 20
Watch: twisting the torso to help the pull, or rounding the lower back — the hinge holds, only the arm moves
9
Calf Raise
Bent-Knee
bodyweight
2 × 8
/ leg
Approximate reference (seated calf raise) — same bent-knee/soleus mechanism, different setup (seated vs. standing on a step)
Target: calves
Position
  • Ball of the foot on the edge of a step, same setup as the straight-leg version
  • Knees bent ~30–40° throughout — this is what shifts the load to the soleus
Execution
  • Rise all the way up, squeeze at the top for 1 sec
  • Lower in 3 seconds — slow eccentric, same cue as the straight-leg version
  • Week 19: bodyweight, 2 sets — held from week 18, volume halved. The soleus is the muscle that fatigues last and quietly on long descents, and it is the one week 18's 2,050 m will have worked hardest, so this stays in as maintenance rather than stimulus. If the knee creeps straight, cut the set short — this exercise earns its work through position, not kilos
Watch: letting the knee straighten mid-set — that shifts the work back to the gastrocnemius and defeats the point of this variant

⚠ Warning signs · stop and reassess
  • Sharp pain (vs. muscle burn) anywhere
  • Lower-back pain after dead bugs / bridges → poor activation, don't add load
  • Knee pain in the split squat or step-up → check alignment or lower height/load
  • Achilles discomfort > 48h → reduce calf-raise volume
  • Still feeling heavy by Sunday → the recovery wasn't deep enough, tell me before week 20 starts
  • Any niggle that survives a full recovery week is a real injury, not fatigue — say so. With three peak weeks next, this is the last cheap moment to find that out
