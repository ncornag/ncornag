⚡ Warm-up · 5 min
Easy bike or jog 5 min → 10 leg swings each direction per leg → 10 bodyweight squats → banded lateral walk (exercise 1) as activation. Two full sessions, Monday and Wednesday — the same grid done twice. Monday stacks after the easy 9 km, Wednesday after the 5 km recovery: run first, lift after. This is the biggest week of the whole block — 62 km and 2,250 m, more of both than you have ever done in seven days. That is exactly why not one number on this page goes up. Every load holds where it has been since week 14. The running is taking all the new stress this week; the gym's job is to keep the joints in line under it, not to add a second progression on top. Two full sessions still happen — that has never been cut and is not being cut in the week that needs them most.
🗓 Wednesday · the second session moved off Thursday
The back half of this week was restructured to buy a rest day: Wednesday 5 km recovery + gym → Thursday 8 km / 400 m of descents → Friday full rest → Saturday 25 km / 1,650 m. The last lift now sits three days out from the mountain instead of two, with nothing but rest between Thursday's descents and Saturday. Everything still depends on you lifting exactly what is written and not one set more — a Wednesday that runs long still taxes 2,050 m of descending spread over Thursday and Saturday. If anything feels off on Wednesday, cut a set — never add one.
Exercises · Monday + Wednesday
1
Banded
Lateral Walk
band · ankles
3 × 15
steps / side
Approximate reference (seated band hip abduction) — same muscle and same band resistance, but yours is standing and walking, which loads the stance leg as well
Target: abductors
Position
  • Band just above the knees, hands on hips
  • Quarter-squat, chest up, feet pointing straight ahead
Execution
  • Step sideways with control, keeping constant tension on the band
  • Resist the trailing leg back in — don't let it snap across
  • Small steps, stay low, no torso lean over the stance leg
  • Week 18: band at the ankles, 3 × 15 steps per side — unchanged, and this is the week it pays for itself. 2,050 m of descending across Friday and Saturday is the single largest downhill load of the block, and a tired glute medius is what lets the knee fall inward on hour four of it. Four minutes, done properly, both sessions
  • It's first on purpose — this is warm-up activation, not a lift; go straight from here into the split squat
Watch: feet turning outward · knees caving inward · standing up out of the quarter-squat. If the knees collapse, the band is too strong — go lighter
2
Bulgarian
Split Squat
12 kg
3 × 10
/ leg
Approximate reference (dumbbell single-leg split squat) — this dataset has no Bulgarian split squat; the pattern is close, the setup differs
Target: quads
Position
  • Back foot on the bench, laces down
  • Front foot forward — knee doesn't pass the toes
Execution
  • Lower until the thigh is ≈ parallel to the floor
  • Torso slightly leaned forward
  • Push through the whole foot, mainly the heel
  • Week 18: 12 kg × 10 reps, lowering in 4 seconds — the load holds and the tempo is the only thing that changed. 12 kg is your ceiling for single-leg work: the next kettlebell is 20 kg, a 67% jump, which is not a progression but an injury. In the biggest running week of the block, the slow lowering is all the extra you need — it is also the exact quality Saturday's descent asks for
Watch: knee caving inward · weight on the back leg · front foot too close. Reps 9 and 10 are where form breaks — stop the set rather than finish it badly
3
Single-Leg
Calf Raise
bodyweight
3 × 8
/ leg
Target: calves
Position
  • Ball of the foot on the edge of a step, heel in the air
  • Hold something for balance, not to support your weight
Execution
  • Rise all the way up, squeeze at the top for 1 sec
  • Lower in 4 seconds — slow eccentric
  • Heel below the step for full range of motion
  • Week 18: bodyweight with the 4s eccentric — held. Your calves and Achilles are about to absorb 2,050 m of braking between Friday and Saturday, which is far more eccentric work than any bell can give them. The gym's job this week is to keep the tissue used to the movement, not to fatigue it — the slow lowering does that on its own. Added load is still not the next step; more reps is
Watch: lowering too fast — brake more than you think you need to. Any Achilles soreness lasting past 48h → cut the volume for the week
4
Single-Leg
Glute Bridge
12 kg
3 × 8
/ leg
Target: glutes
Position
  • Lying on your back, one knee bent, heel close to your glutes
  • The other leg straight or with the knee pulled to your chest
Execution
  • Week 18: 12 kg, hold 3 sec at the top — held, and 12 kg is the ceiling here too. Strong glutes are what stop your quads doing all the braking on a long descent. With 1,650 m of it coming on Saturday, this is the most race-specific exercise on the page — do not rush the hold
  • Drive through the heel of the supporting leg, raise your hip
  • Hips level — don't let the free side drop
  • Squeeze the glute, hold 2 sec at the top
  • Ribs down — don't arch your lower back
  • If the weight breaks hip level, drop back to 6 kg — form rules
Watch: the free-side hip drops or rotates → shorten the range and level out before continuing. Drive from the glute, never the lower back
5
Dead
Bug
bodyweight
3 × 8
/ side
Target: abs
Position
  • Lying on your back, arms straight up toward the ceiling
  • Knees at 90° over your hips (tabletop)
Execution
  • Lower back pressed to the floor — the key point
  • Slowly extend the opposite arm + leg
  • Without touching the floor, return, switch sides
  • Flat back > range of motion
  • Week 18: 4 sec per rep. Unchanged, still bodyweight. This one has never taken load and never will — it is a control drill, and it progresses through tempo and a longer lever. On a 62 km week it is also the cheapest thing here: two minutes, no fatigue cost
Watch: this is anti-extension control, not strength — adding weight recruits the psoas and arches your back. Back arching as you extend the leg → reduce the range
6
Side
Plank
bodyweight
3 × 40s
/ side
Approximate reference (incline side plank variant) — this dataset has no plain side-plank hold
Target: abs
Position
  • Side plank on your forearm
  • Body in a straight line from head to feet
  • Hips stacked, not rotated or dropped
  • Bottom leg on the floor, top leg stacked on top
Execution
  • Contract your obliques and core, keep a straight line
  • Breathe, don't hold your breath
  • Week 18: 40 sec — held. The clock is the only thing that progresses here, and the biggest running week of the block is not where you move it
  • Cut the time short if your hips drop — form > duration
Watch: hips dropping or rotating — cut the time before your form breaks
7
High Step-up
+ Eccentric Descent
bodyweight
3 × 8
/ leg
Approximate reference (dumbbell step-up) — shows the step-up pattern only; doesn't capture the box height or slow eccentric descent that define this exercise
Target: glutes
Position
  • Use the jump box — height at mid-shin / knee
  • Whole foot planted on top, not just the toes
Execution
  • Step up driving through the heel of the top leg, no push-off from the bottom leg
  • Drive the knee up — mimics the power-hike motion on a climb
  • Knee tracking over the foot — not caving inward
  • Lower in 4 seconds — the slow eccentric preps your quads for trail descents
  • Week 18: bodyweight, box height back up to your full step. This is the promised step and the only real progression on the page — height is this movement's progression, never load. It is also the closest thing on this page to Saturday itself: 1,650 m of controlled lowering, one leg at a time. Take the full 4 seconds down on every rep; if the last set gets sloppy, stop it there rather than finish it badly
Watch: the value is in the slow descent and the higher box, not stepping up fast. Knee pain → lower the height or reduce the range
8
One-Arm
Bent-Over Row
12 kg
3 × 12
/ arm
Target: upper back
Position
  • Hinge at the hips, flat back, soft knees
  • Free hand on a bench or your thigh for support
  • Kettlebell hangs straight down from the working arm
Execution
  • Pull the kettlebell to your hip, elbow close to the body
  • Squeeze the shoulder blade at the top
  • Lower under control, don't let it drop
  • Week 18: 12 kg × 12 reps — held. Your back does no braking on a descent, so this is the one movement the biggest week does not need to protect — but it is also not the week to test a new load with a 25 km Saturday two days out. It stays at 12 kg because that is the heaviest single bell you can row cleanly; when 12 × 12 becomes genuinely easy, the next step is both bells together, 18 kg — not the 20 kg on its own
Watch: twisting the torso to help the pull, or rounding the lower back — the hinge holds, only the arm moves
9
Calf Raise
Bent-Knee
bodyweight
3 × 8
/ leg
Approximate reference (seated calf raise) — same bent-knee/soleus mechanism, different setup (seated vs. standing on a step)
Target: calves
Position
  • Ball of the foot on the edge of a step, same setup as the straight-leg version
  • Knees bent ~30–40° throughout — this is what shifts the load to the soleus
Execution
  • Rise all the way up, squeeze at the top for 1 sec
  • Lower in 3 seconds — slow eccentric, same cue as the straight-leg version
  • Week 18: bodyweight — held, provided the knee has been staying bent through all three sets. The soleus is the muscle that fatigues last and quietly on long descents — and Saturday is the longest descent of the block. The position is doing its job; adding load three days out would only borrow from Saturday
Watch: letting the knee straighten mid-set — that shifts the work back to the gastrocnemius and defeats the point of this variant

⚠ Warning signs · stop and reassess
  • Sharp pain (vs. muscle burn) anywhere
  • Lower-back pain after dead bugs / bridges → poor activation, don't add load
  • Knee pain in the split squat or step-up → check alignment or lower height/load
  • Achilles discomfort > 48h → reduce calf-raise volume
  • Quads still sore on Wednesday → cut a set and lower the box; do not push through it into Thursday
  • Legs not right on Thursday → shorten Thursday's descents first, then Saturday's route; don't cancel either
  • Anything sharp in the knees on the descent → walk the rest of it; that is the injury this whole programme exists to prevent
